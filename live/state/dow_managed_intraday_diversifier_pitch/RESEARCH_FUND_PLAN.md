# Dow Managed Intraday Diversifier Research Funding Plan

**Status:** internal planning draft for counsel/accountant review.  
**Purpose:** create a legitimate, non-commercial research funding framework to validate the Dow Managed Intraday Diversifier Strategy without presenting it as a client investment product, pooled fund, CTA program, or managed account service.  
**Not legal advice:** every structural, tax, securities, derivatives, and cross-border item below must be reviewed by qualified counsel before money is accepted or traded.

## Executive Summary

The goal is not to launch a public CTA or investment fund yet. The goal is to create a tightly documented **12-month proprietary R&D sandbox** that can:

- fund software development and execution infrastructure,
- trade only the corporation's own research capital,
- validate live-vs-replay execution quality,
- prove broker-paper parity before larger deployment,
- produce a clean audit packet for a future registered CTA / CTC / managed-account launch.

The safest naming is **Research Funding Program** or **R&D Grant Pool**, not "fund," "investment pool," "managed account," or "client allocation." The core legal distinction is that contributors should not receive redemption rights, profit distributions, performance fees, managed-account control, or advice. They are funding software R&D, not investing in a trading vehicle.

## Core Principle

This phase must be structurally non-commercial.

Allowed:

- fixed software-development compensation,
- corporate proprietary trading using the corporation's own treasury,
- internal research reporting,
- broker-paper validation,
- live telemetry collection,
- post-project review to decide whether to pursue registration.

Not allowed in this phase unless counsel creates a registered/exempt structure:

- management fees,
- incentive/performance fees,
- "client" language,
- "AUM" language,
- redemption promises,
- profit shares,
- discretionary trading of third-party accounts,
- pooled investor capital,
- advertising performance to solicit commodity-interest accounts,
- representing backtests as live/audited performance.

## Recommended Structure

### Entity

Create a dedicated Canadian private corporation for software engineering and quantitative research.

Working description:

> A Canadian-controlled private corporation focused on software engineering, data analytics, execution infrastructure, and proprietary quantitative research.

Recommended first counsel question:

> Should this be federally incorporated with an Ontario registered office, provincially incorporated in Ontario, or formed under another structure given the founder's residence, contributors' residence, brokerage access, tax treatment, and future CTA/CTC plans?

### Why A Corporation

- Separates the developer personally from the research capital.
- Lets the broker account be opened as a corporate proprietary account.
- Creates corporate books, board resolutions, and bank/broker records.
- Makes the R&D/software purpose easier to document.
- Keeps the future commercial product path cleaner.
- May allow accountant review of SR&ED / R&D treatment, subject to eligibility.

### Activity Restriction

The corporate purpose should be narrow enough that a reviewer does not mistake the entity for an unregistered manager.

Draft restriction for counsel review:

> The Corporation's activities are restricted to software engineering, quantitative data analytics, and experimental development of automated execution architectures. The Corporation is prohibited from soliciting public investor capital, operating a pooled investment vehicle, offering wealth management services, or acting as an investment adviser or commodity trading adviser to third parties unless and until all required registrations or exemptions are obtained.

## Funding Terms

### Required Economic Terms

The R&D agreement should say:

- the contribution is non-refundable,
- the contribution is non-redeemable,
- the contributor receives no trading profit,
- the contributor receives no account equity,
- the contributor receives no right to direct trades,
- the contributor receives no interest, coupon, or return target,
- the contributor receives no ownership of the strategy,
- any live trading gains remain corporate treasury,
- any live trading losses are research costs,
- developer compensation is fixed and not performance-based.

### Contributor Name

Use:

- contributor,
- grantor,
- research sponsor,
- family/friends R&D sponsor.

Avoid:

- investor,
- LP,
- client,
- account holder,
- subscriber,
- allocator,
- managed-account customer.

### Contribution Instrument

Counsel should choose one:

1. **Non-refundable research grant.** Cleanest conceptually, but must be tax-reviewed.
2. **Corporate equity investment.** Cleaner corporate-law ownership, but may trigger securities-offering requirements and creates investor-rights issues.
3. **Shareholder loan.** Easier accounting, but repayment expectations can blur the "no redemption" idea.
4. **Services/prepayment contract.** Could work if contributor is sponsoring software R&D deliverables, but must not promise investment returns.

Working recommendation: **research grant or corporate equity**, after counsel/tax review. Avoid anything that sounds like a deposit into a trading account.

## Regulatory Guardrails

### US Futures / NFA / CFTC

Official NFA guidance defines a CTA as someone who, **for compensation or profit**, advises others about futures, options on futures, retail off-exchange forex, or swaps. It also says registered CTAs that manage/discretion customer accounts or give tailored commodity advice generally must be NFA Members. NFA also lists registration steps such as Form 7-R, membership application, fees, Form 8-R for principals/APs, fingerprints, and proficiency requirements.

Research implication:

- Do not advise contributors about futures trading.
- Do not take POA over contributor accounts.
- Do not trade contributor accounts.
- Do not charge compensation for commodity trading advice.
- Do not hold out publicly as a CTA during the research phase.
- Treat CTA registration as the default path before any commercial client product.

NFA notes that CFTC Regulation 4.14 contains CTA registration exemption criteria, including advice to 15 or fewer persons in the past 12 months and not generally holding out as a CTA. Do not rely on this without counsel.

### NFA Disclosure Documents

NFA says CTAs that are registered or required to register must provide disclosure documents to prospective clients unless an exemption applies, and such documents must be filed with NFA before use.

Research implication:

- The R&D sponsor deck must not be a CTA disclosure document.
- Do not use the pitch as client solicitation material.
- If moving to client accounts later, create a new counsel-reviewed/NFA-reviewed disclosure document.

### NFA Promotional Material / Hypothetical Performance

NFA Rule 2-29 restricts deceptive promotional material, requires risk discussion beside profit discussion, and prescribes hypothetical performance disclaimers. It also addresses comparable information and restrictions around hypothetical performance once actual trading exists.

Research implication:

- Every performance page must say hypothetical/backtested.
- Do not imply likely profits.
- Keep support files for every number.
- Once live telemetry exists, segregate it from historical backtests.
- Do not show cherry-picked extracted performance without counsel review.

### US Securities / Regulation D

If the research contribution is structured as equity, notes, membership interests, profit interests, SAFEs, or any other security, US securities rules may matter for US persons. SEC guidance says many private offering exemptions limit participation to accredited investors or restrict non-accredited participation. Investor.gov notes Rule 506(b) prohibits general solicitation, allows unlimited accredited investors and up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited purchasers, and requires false/misleading statements to be avoided. Rule 506(c) permits general solicitation only if all purchasers are accredited investors and verification steps are taken.

Research implication:

- Do not publicly solicit.
- Do not use social media, web forms, or broad marketing.
- Avoid US contributors unless counsel sets the exemption path.
- If accepting US contributors, evaluate accredited status, Form D, state notice filings, and anti-fraud disclosure obligations.

### Canada / Ontario

The attached notes identify the OSC "business trigger" concern. Counsel must confirm whether the structure avoids registration requirements under Ontario securities/derivatives law and whether a Canadian commodity trading manager/adviser registration issue exists.

Research implication:

- Keep the activity proprietary.
- No client accounts.
- No advisory compensation.
- No investment returns.
- No public solicitation.
- No redemptions.
- No pooled investment language.
- Ensure the corporation is not "in the business" of trading/advising for others.

### Broker / FCM Account

The corporate broker account must be opened as a proprietary research account if possible.

Broker questions:

- Will StoneX/Tradovate open a Canadian corporate futures account?
- Can the account trade MYM/MNQ/NQ in sim and live?
- Are API orders permitted?
- Are server-side OCO/OSO brackets available and auditable?
- Are account statements sufficient for a future track record packet?
- Can separate sim and live accounts be maintained?
- Are market-data entitlements corporate or individual?

## 12-Month Roadmap

### Month 0: Legal / Entity Setup

Deliverables:

- counsel consult,
- accountant consult,
- entity formed,
- business bank account opened,
- grant/instrument selected,
- board resolutions signed,
- research agreement signed,
- broker-account application started,
- data vendor plan approved,
- internal compliance binder created.

Gate to proceed:

- no funds accepted until counsel signs off on the structure.

### Months 1-3: Build The Rails

Technical deliverables:

- Tradovate adapter smoke test,
- Tradovate market-data feed shadow mode,
- Tradovate broker-routing adapter in demo only,
- server-side OCO/OSO validation,
- emergency flatten command,
- runtime supervisor modes,
- broker/local reconciliation,
- restart recovery,
- raw event persistence,
- feed quality logs,
- no-secrets repo hygiene.

Research deliverables:

- replay assumptions register,
- same-bar ambiguity audit,
- missing-minute forward-fill policy,
- slippage/spread policy,
- unit test coverage for fault states,
- baseline report template.

Gate to proceed:

- no live trading until demo order lifecycle is reconciled end-to-end.

### Months 4-6: Paper The System

Activities:

- run live market-data shadow sessions,
- run broker demo/sim sessions,
- compare broker-paper fills to replay fills,
- record order-ack latency,
- test reconnect during RTH,
- test emergency flatten during sim,
- test stale-feed freeze,
- test local/broker divergence overwrite,
- test end-of-day flatten,
- test kill switch under stress.

Success metrics:

- 100% order/fill/position reconciliation at EOD,
- no duplicate entries after restart,
- no missing RTH bars after normalization,
- protective orders active server-side or strategy blocked,
- unresolved order ambiguity rate near zero,
- all faults produce written event logs.

Gate to proceed:

- 30 consecutive paper sessions without critical reconciliation failure.

### Months 7-12: Minimum-Size Live Telemetry Pilot

Purpose:

- collect real broker fills,
- measure slippage against paper/replay,
- verify live operational behavior,
- avoid optimizing based on live outcomes.

Sizing:

- Use the smallest contract expression counsel/operator approves.
- If the full research strategy requires a 5-contract MYM base book, consider a reduced execution-telemetry variant first. The goal is not maximizing P&L; the goal is proving the plumbing.

Hard controls:

- daily max loss threshold,
- weekly max loss threshold,
- historical drawdown review threshold,
- stale-feed freeze,
- broker-divergence freeze,
- manual kill switch,
- no overnight unmanaged positions,
- no new entries if OCO/OSO not confirmed.

Success metrics:

- live fill distribution is no better than replay assumptions,
- live slippage is within predefined tolerance,
- all live trades reconstruct from raw events,
- no unreconciled orders,
- no unexplained positions,
- no manual intervention required except planned drills,
- realized live behavior reaches at least 80% of modeled execution quality after costs.

Failure metrics:

- any unflattened unexpected position,
- any unresolved live order after timeout,
- any duplicate entry after restart,
- any protective order not accepted while position is live,
- repeated stale-feed conditions during RTH,
- live slippage/fees reduce modeled edge below viability,
- drawdown exceeds the research stop threshold,
- operator cannot explain a fill from logged state.
